A B+ tree keeps its records in sorted leaf pages. Suppose a leaf can hold four keys and all four slots are full. Inserting a fifth key leaves us with two jobs: make room for it and give future searches a way to find the right leaf.

<LeafSplit />

## How the separator works

With a separator of `36`, the root sends a search for `25` left and a search for `44` right. A search for `36` itself also goes right. The parent has a copy of the separator, while the record for `36` stays in the right leaf.

Try inserting `58` and step to the end. The separator is now `44`. The key you inserted can differ from the key the root uses to route searches.

## What the example leaves out

Real pages are usually measured in bytes. Variable-length records can make an even split by key count a poor choice. A split can also fill an existing parent and propagate upward. This example starts with a single root leaf and uses a fixed capacity so you can follow one split at a time.

The demo runs the sorted insertion and split calculation in Rust compiled to WebAssembly. TypeScript renders each step and handles the controls.
